What It Is

Gallstones form when substances in bile harden inside the gallbladder. Many cause no symptoms, but others lead to pain, particularly after fatty food, or to more serious complications if a stone blocks the bile duct.

Symptoms

  • Sudden pain in the upper right or centre of the abdomen, often after eating
  • Pain spreading to the back or shoulder
  • Nausea or vomiting
  • Yellowing of the skin or eyes, fever, or dark urine in more serious cases

Causes & Risk Factors

Gallstones are more common with increasing age, in women, during pregnancy, with a family history of gallstones, and with rapid weight loss or being overweight.

Diagnosis

Usually confirmed with an ultrasound scan, sometimes alongside blood tests to check for infection or blockage.

Treatment Options

Ranges from monitoring for stones causing no problems, to removal of the gallbladder using a laparoscopic or robotic approach where symptoms or complications make surgery the right option.
